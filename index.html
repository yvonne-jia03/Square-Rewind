Where This Comes From

I grew up in this problem.

My family owns Tea House, a Chinese restaurant in Minneapolis. Open since 1997, serving 700+ customers a day — it is the backbone of what rooted us in this country. It paid for my education, my home, my clothes. For years, I watched my mother and my aunt close at 11pm and then sit down at the kitchen table with a stack of receipts, a notepad, and a calculator. Not because they didn't care about their data — but because no tool existed that spoke their language, literally or figuratively.

"The hardest part of running a restaurant isn't the food. It's the paperwork at the end of the day when you're already exhausted and just want to go home."

I ran operations at Tea House for five years. I know exactly what closes get missed, what data gets lost, and what decisions get made on gut instead of numbers — not because they were unsophisticated, but because the efficient tools used by large-scale restaurants didn't speak the same language and didn't understand the needs of the small, local ones.

The Problem

Three walls between small restaurant owners and their own data.

01

Cognitive Overload

After a 14-hour shift, most owners don't have the bandwidth to cross-reference sales, labor, inventory, and cash. So the reconciliation doesn't get done — or gets done wrong.

02

Language Barrier

Over 30% of U.S. restaurant owners are immigrants. For many, dense English-language reporting interfaces are inaccessible — making it nearly impossible to self-serve or ask for help.

03

Data Literacy Gap

Existing tools output raw numbers, not decisions. Owners know something is off but can't pinpoint why. Data that isn't understood doesn't drive action — it just accumulates.

These three problems compound each other. An exhausted owner who can't read the interface and doesn't understand the output will eventually stop looking at the data entirely — and make expensive decisions in the dark. The tools that exist were built for large-scale restaurants — they didn't speak the same language, and didn't understand the needs of the small, local ones.

The Solution

Square Rewind

AI-Native Feature

One tap. One summary. Every night.

Square Rewind is an AI-powered end-of-day digest built directly into Square for Restaurants. At close, it automatically synthesizes the day's POS data, labor costs, and inventory movement into a plain-language summary — no manual input, no spreadsheets, no reconciliation.

It tells owners what happened, what to watch, and what to do tomorrow. In their language. In under 60 seconds.

AI Architecture

Why this is only possible with AI.

Square Rewind isn't a dashboard. It's a reasoning layer on top of existing Square data — turning numbers into narrative, anomalies into alerts, and patterns into predictions.

🧠

Natural Language Generation

LLM-powered summaries convert raw POS, payroll, and inventory data into plain-language daily rewinds — written at a reading level accessible to any owner, regardless of background.

🌐

Multilingual Output

Every digest is available in 10+ languages with one tap — directly addressing the language barrier that locks immigrant owners out of understanding their own business data.

📡

Anomaly Detection

ML models flag statistical deviations in real time — cash discrepancies, unusual labor spikes, inventory burn rates — before they become costly problems.

📈

Predictive Recommendations

Pattern recognition across historical data generates forward-looking suggestions: prep quantities, staffing for the next day, items to 86 early — getting smarter with each close.

Rollout

How I'd ship it.

Phase 1 — Beta (Weeks 1–6): Launch to 500 opted-in Square for Restaurants users in markets with high immigrant ownership concentration (NYC, LA, Houston, Minneapolis). English + Mandarin + Spanish only. Collect digest open rates, feature feedback, and anomaly alert accuracy.

Phase 2 — Iteration (Weeks 7–12): Expand language support to 10 languages based on user distribution. Tune LLM summary quality based on feedback. Add predictive recommendations layer.

Phase 3 — General Availability: Roll out to all Square for Restaurants users. Explore integration with Square Loans — surfacing cash flow patterns that could inform loan eligibility or timing.

The Square Loans integration is the long-term monetization unlock: an owner who understands their cash flow through Square Rewind is a better candidate for — and more likely to trust — a Square business loan.
